# Guest Management

> Complete guide to finding, booking, preparing, and following up with podcast guests.

## Guest Pipeline Stages

```
Research → Shortlist → Outreach → Confirm → Prep → Record → Follow-Up → Archive
```

## 1. Research & Sourcing

### Where to Find Guests
- Industry conferences and speaker lists
- Other podcasts in your niche (guest cross-pollination)
- LinkedIn thought leaders and content creators
- Book authors (promote new releases = mutual value)
- Community members and audience suggestions
- Twitter/X conversations and threads

### Guest Evaluation Criteria
| Criteria | Weight | Description |
|----------|--------|-------------|
| Topic fit | High | Does their expertise match your episode plan? |
| Communication | High | Are they articulate and engaging? |
| Audience reach | Medium | Will they share the episode? |
| Availability | Medium | Can they record within your timeline? |
| Uniqueness | Medium | Fresh perspective vs repeat of common takes |

## 2. Outreach

### Cold Outreach Template
```
Subject: Podcast Interview — [Topic] on [Show Name]

Hi [Name],

I'm the host of [Show Name], a podcast about [topic] with [audience size/description].

I'd love to have you on to discuss [specific angle — NOT just "your work"].

The conversation would be [duration] and we record via [platform]. We handle all editing and promotion.

Recent episodes: [link to 1-2 relevant episodes]

Would you be interested?

Best,
[Your name]
```

### Follow-Up Cadence
- Day 0: Send initial outreach
- Day 5: First follow-up (if no response)
- Day 14: Second follow-up (different angle or shorter ask)
- Day 21: Close the loop ("No worries if timing doesn't work")

### Warm Introductions
- Use `guest-research` skill to find mutual connections
- Ask existing guests for referrals
- Engage with potential guests on social media before pitching

## 3. Booking & Scheduling

### Confirmation Email Should Include
- Recording date, time, and timezone
- Expected duration (default: 45 minutes + 15 min buffer)
- Platform and link (send 24h before and 1h before)
- Topic and angle (not full questions — keep it conversational)
- Technical requirements (microphone, quiet room, wired internet)
- Release timeline ("Episode will air approximately [date]")

### Guest Calendar
Use the `episode-schedule` skill to coordinate:
- Record at least **1 week before** target publish date
- Batch recordings when possible (2-3 in one day)
- Block **3 days before** recording for guest prep

## 4. Pre-Recording Prep

### Guest Brief (send 48-72 hours before)
Use `guest-prep.md` prompt to generate:
- Episode topic and angle
- 3-5 broad topic areas (not specific questions)
- Technical setup checklist
- What to expect during recording

### Host Prep
- Review all guest research
- Prepare 15 questions using `interview-questions.md` prompt
- Identify 2-3 "highlight moment" targets
- Know when to deviate from the script

## 5. Day of Recording

### Pre-Recording (15 min before)
- Welcome guest, make them comfortable
- Quick audio check (ask them to speak for 30 seconds)
- Confirm pronunciation of their name
- Remind them: "Feel free to re-answer any question"
- Start recording **before** the conversation officially begins

### During Recording
- Listen actively — follow the interesting threads
- Don't rush to the next question
- Let silence happen (guests often fill it with gold)
- Note timestamps of great moments for clips

## 6. Post-Recording Follow-Up

### Within 24 Hours
- Thank you email with expected timeline
- Share any relevant links discussed during recording

### At Publication
- Send guest the episode link, show notes, and clips
- Tag them in all social media promotion
- Provide pre-written social copy they can share

### 2 Days After Publication
- Check engagement and share metrics with guest
- Ask for a review/rating if relationship is strong
- Invite them back for a future episode (if it went well)

## Guest Red Flags
- Won't use a microphone ("my laptop mic is fine")
- Wants to approve the final edit
- Only wants to promote their product
- Can't commit to a recording time
- Hasn't listened to a single episode
