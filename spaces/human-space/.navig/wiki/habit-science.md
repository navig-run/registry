# Habit Science Reference

> Based on *Atomic Habits* by James Clear and behavioral psychology research.

## The Four Laws of Behavior Change

| Law | To Build | To Break |
|-----|----------|----------|
| **1st Law: Cue** | Make it obvious | Make it invisible |
| **2nd Law: Craving** | Make it attractive | Make it unattractive |
| **3rd Law: Response** | Make it easy | Make it difficult |
| **4th Law: Reward** | Make it satisfying | Make it unsatisfying |

## Implementation Intentions

**Formula**: "I will [BEHAVIOR] at [TIME] in [LOCATION]."

**Habit Stacking**: "After I [CURRENT HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT]."

Examples:
- "After I pour my morning coffee, I will meditate for 2 minutes."
- "After I sit at my desk, I will write my top 3 priorities."
- "After I finish dinner, I will journal for 5 minutes."

## The Two-Minute Rule

When starting a new habit, reduce it to a two-minute version:
- "Read for 30 minutes" → "Read one page"
- "Do 30 minutes of yoga" → "Roll out the yoga mat"
- "Study for the exam" → "Open my notes"

## Identity-Based Habits

Instead of outcome-based goals, focus on identity:
- ❌ "I want to lose weight" → ✅ "I am someone who moves every day"
- ❌ "I want to read more" → ✅ "I am a reader"
- ❌ "I want to be organized" → ✅ "I am someone who keeps their space tidy"

Each habit completion is a vote for your new identity.

## The Habit Portfolio (Max 5 Active)

Rules for managing your habit portfolio:
1. **Maximum 5 active habits** — Focus creates mastery
2. **Graduate habits** — When a habit becomes automatic (30+ day streak, feels effortless), graduate it and add a new one
3. **Monthly review** — First of each month, audit the portfolio
4. **Drop before add** — If at 5, graduate or drop one before adding

## Environment Design

Your environment is the invisible hand that shapes behavior:
- **Make cues visible**: Place habit triggers where you'll see them
- **Reduce friction**: Prepare everything in advance (gym clothes, journal open, water bottle filled)
- **Remove competing cues**: Hide distractions (phone in another room, TV unplugged)
- **Designate spaces**: One space = one purpose (desk for focus, chair for reading)

## Recovery: The Never-Miss-Twice Rule

Everyone misses a day. The rule:
1. **Missing once is an accident**. Missing twice is the start of a new pattern.
2. If you miss, do the **two-minute version** the next day. Just show up.
3. Identify what caused the miss: Was it the **cue**, the **routine**, or the **environment**?
4. Adjust one thing and restart.
